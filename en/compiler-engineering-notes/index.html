Compiler Engineering Notes: 48 Bilingual Articles and Reading Paths

Numerics, operators, resources, and compiler delivery

Posted by Bruce Lee on 2026-01-02

阅读中文版目录

About the dates: blog dates follow the editorial reading order from January to September 2026, roughly one article every five or six days. They are not dates of commits, incidents, or experiments. Both editions use the same editorial dates.

48 long-form articles on compiler numerics, layouts, scheduling, and delivery. The stories often start with a small question: why does computing a mean require moving data? Why can a program not find a file that exists? Why does a view keep memory alive? Why does Debug work while Release fails?

These articles draw on code, tests, and design records from real development history, then explain the underlying ideas through generic concepts and redesigned examples. Read the four parts in sequence or start with the problem you are facing. Article IDs organize topics; they are not a commit chronology.

This is the complete English counterpart of the Chinese collection. Each article retains its original ID and filename so the two editions can be matched directly. The English edition is self-contained.

How to read the stories and evidence

A reconstructed teaching scenario uses redesigned characters, dialogue, inputs, and investigation sequences to explain an issue. It is not a verbatim historical account. The articles distinguish facts supported by the reviewed code, inferences drawn from those facts, and proposed experiments.

Reading test source establishes what a test intends to check, not that it passed in the current environment. The original project, device tests, and performance benchmarks were not run while preparing this collection. Performance hypotheses are therefore not presented as measurements. Candidate designs in a specification do not automatically become implemented operator capabilities.

The articles retain general formulas, engineering invariants, counterexamples, and tradeoffs. Project names, personal identities, host paths, commit identifiers, and proprietary encoding details have been removed from the public material.

Follow a question across the series

  • Can this rewrite be removed? N04 → N10 → R10: mathematical identity, result representation, and optimization preconditions must all hold.
  • Is the observed error the real error? O04 → E09: debugging layouts and test baselines can be wrong too.
  • Do fewer nodes always mean faster execution? N07 → O10 → O11: movement, memory, rounding boundaries, and consumers determine the potential benefit.
  • What does an operator name promise? E11 → E14 → E15: a name and an output shape leave much of the numerical contract unspecified.
  • Does a file’s existence mean success? E01 → E06 → E07 → R07: required side effects and output states need verifiable completion conditions.
